How we compute your numbers

Every payment formula in LoanPilot comes from the text of the law that created these plans — Public Law 119-21 (July 2025) and the Higher Education Act — not from someone else's calculator. We cross-checked our results against three independent sources (the EDCAP nonprofit's calculator, Student Loan Planner, and The College Investor); they agree with ours to the cent.

RAP (Repayment Assistance Plan)

  • Your yearly base amount is a percentage of your adjusted gross income (AGI): 1% if your AGI is between $10,001 and $20,000, rising by one point for each $10,000, up to 10% above $100,000. At or below $10,000 AGI the base is $120.
  • Your monthly payment is that base divided by 12, minus $50 for each dependent you claim on your taxes — but never less than $10/month.
  • If your payment doesn't cover the month's interest, the rest of that interest is waived, so your balance never grows under RAP.
  • If your payment reduces your principal by less than $50 in a month, the government tops up the principal reduction — by the difference between $50 (or your full payment, if it's smaller) and the principal you actually paid.
  • Whatever is left after 360 qualifying monthly payments (30 years) is cancelled.

IBR (Income-Based Repayment)

  • We subtract 150% of the federal poverty guideline for your family size (2026 figures: $15,960 + $5,680 per additional person, contiguous US) from your AGI. That's your "discretionary income."
  • New borrowers (no federal loans before July 1, 2014) pay 10% of it, spread over 12 months, with forgiveness after 20 years. Earlier borrowers pay 15%, with forgiveness after 25 years.
  • Your IBR payment is capped at what you'd pay on a 10-year Standard plan at the income you had when you entered IBR.

Standard and Tiered Standard

  • The classic Standard plan spreads each loan over 10 years of equal payments at your interest rate.
  • For loans first disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, the new Standard plan picks your term from your total balance: under $25,000 → 10 years; $25,000 to under $50,000 → 15 years; $50,000 to under $100,000 → 20 years; $100,000 or more → 25 years.

Assumptions in every projection

  • Your income stays flat and every payment arrives on time. Real life will differ; recalculate when your income changes.
  • We round to whole cents at each step of the math, rounding half-cents away from zero.
  • Multiple loans are simulated together at a balance-weighted rate for RAP and IBR projections; fixed plans amortize each loan separately.
  • Poverty figures are the contiguous-US table; Alaska and Hawaii guidelines are higher, so IBR estimates there run slightly high.
  • Estimates only — not financial advice. Confirm with your servicer before switching plans (plan switches can be one-way doors).
